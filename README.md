## Overview

**Śruti** literally means **“that which is heard.”** In many Hindu traditions, it refers to revealed knowledge preserved through the Vedas and regarded as the highest scriptural authority.

This page is designed as a polished starting point for study, documentation, and future expansion into a larger knowledge archive.

---

## Meaning

Śruti is commonly understood as:

- **That which is heard**
- **Revealed Vedic knowledge**
- **Highest scriptural authority** in many Hindu traditions

It stands at the center of the Vedic textual world and serves as a foundational reference for ritual, philosophy, and theology.

## The Four Vedas

### Rigveda

The **Rigveda** is the oldest Veda and is primarily a collection of hymns.

**Nature**
- Oldest Veda
- Hymn collection
- Praise of deities and cosmic order

**Major Themes**
- Agni
- Indra
- Varuna
- Mitra
- Cosmic creation

**Associated Literature**
- Rigveda Samhita
- Aitareya Brahmana
- Kaushitaki Brahmana
- Aitareya Aranyaka
- Aitareya Upanishad

### Yajurveda

The **Yajurveda** is closely linked to ritual performance and sacrificial procedure.

**Nature**
- Ritual formulas
- Sacrificial procedures
- Manual for yajna performance

**Major Branches**
- Krishna Yajurveda
- Shukla Yajurveda

**Krishna Yajurveda**
- Taittiriya Samhita
- Taittiriya Brahmana
- Taittiriya Aranyaka
- Taittiriya Upanishad

**Shukla Yajurveda**
- Vajasaneyi Samhita
- Shatapatha Brahmana
- Brihadaranyaka Upanishad
- Isha Upanishad

### Samaveda

The **Samaveda** is the Veda of melody, chant, and sacred song.

**Nature**
- Veda of melody and chant
- Liturgical singing
- Musical arrangement of Vedic verses

**Major Features**
- Many verses drawn from the Rigveda
- Chanted in ritual contexts
- Foundation for sacred song tradition

**Associated Literature**
- Samaveda Samhita
- Panchavimsha Brahmana
- Jaiminiya Brahmana
- Chandogya Upanishad
- Kena Upanishad

### Atharvaveda

The **Atharvaveda** focuses strongly on practical, domestic, and protective concerns, while also containing philosophical material.

**Nature**
- Practical and domestic concerns
- Healing and protection
- Social and household life
- Philosophical hymns also present

**Major Themes**
- Medicine
- Spells and remedies
- Protection from harm
- Kingship and social order
- Early speculative thought

**Associated Literature**
- Atharvaveda Samhita
- Gopatha Brahmana
- Mundaka Upanishad
- Mandukya Upanishad
- Prashna Upanishad

## Importance of Śruti

Śruti is central because it serves as:

- The primary authority for Vedic traditions
- A source for later interpretation
- The basis for ritual, philosophy, and theology
- The foundation for Vedanta

It functions both as a scriptural origin point and as a continuing reference for deeper inquiry.

---

## Related but Distinct from Śruti

Śruti is distinct from other major categories of Hindu literature and tradition:

- Smriti
- Itihasa
- Purana
- Dharmaśāstra
- Vedangas
- Upavedas

These are closely related to the broader Vedic world, but they are not Śruti itself.
